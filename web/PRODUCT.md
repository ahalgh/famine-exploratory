# Product

**Famine Explorer** is an internal research tool for exploring food-security early-warning data: FEWS NET IPC phases by livelihood zone, travel-time accessibility, market price coverage and the coverage of every input dataset, for Guatemala, Kenya, Malawi, Nigeria, Somalia and Uganda.

## Who uses it

Researchers and analysts on this project, working at a desk in long sessions while they build and test early-warning models. They know IPC phases and FEWS NET terminology. They use it to orient themselves before analysis, not to report to the public.

## Jobs

1. **Which zones are worst off, and how remote are they?** Pick a country, read the ranked zones beside the map, and click one for its IPC history and travel times. This is the main job.
2. **What data exists where and when?** Check which datasets cover a country and which months are missing before trusting an analysis.
3. **Where are the markets?** See WFP and FAO price locations against the zones.

## Mode

Operate (Impeccable). It's an app screen people work in (Taste-skill's app-screen rules): restraint, earned familiarity and scannability come before expression.

## Truths the interface must keep

- IPC phases use the official IPC colours (1 `#cdfacd`, 2 `#fae61e`, 3 `#e67800`, 4 `#c80000`, 5 `#640000`).
- Caveats travel with the numbers. Travel times are for ranking, not literal hours. Travel time to a WFP market partly reflects WFP presence. Nigeria 2019 zones use an older map and are not drawn.
- Missing values print as a dash and real zeros as zeros. Findings are computed from the data on screen, never written by hand.
